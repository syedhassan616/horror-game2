# 01 — The Companions

Five characters who are, mechanically, Aven's second body. Each dossier is complete
against the twelve-field schema.

---

## TILLY BRACK — ANCHOR

> *"The count has to be right."*

**History.** Eleven. Wyndmarrow-born, third generation on the rope walk. Her grandmother
was a bell-hanger and Tilly has her tools. Mother **Odile** severed week 2 of the Ninth
Quiet; father **Halden** severed week 4. Tilly rings both their bells every dawn using a
three-rope arrangement she designed herself, which works, and which no adult in the village
has examined closely enough to realise is *good*.

**Goals.** *Today:* ring three bells before the board is updated. *This act:* be believed
by an adult. *Overall:* not be sent somewhere kinder.

**Fears.** That the count was always wrong and nobody checked. Being carried instead of
carrying. Sleeping in.

**Personality.** Relentlessly literal. Corrects adults. Enormous competence in a narrow
band — bells, ropes, knots, weather, load-bearing — and almost none outside it. **Not
precocious:** she is a child doing an adult's job badly and refusing help, and the gap
between her competence and her judgement is the character.

**Dialogue style.** Short declaratives. Refuses metaphor:
> *"That's not what a heart is. A heart is a muscle. I've seen a sheep's."*

Heavy `[INTERRUPT]` use — she talks over adults constantly, and adults let her, and that
is its own small tragedy. **Tell: she never says how she feels; she says what needs doing
instead.** Writers must never give her an emotional statement. Her feelings are always
expressed as a task.

**Relationships.** Odile (unbearable) · Halden (she has started managing him) · Guisley
(grudging apprenticeship; he is the only adult who tests her work) · Moth (deeply
unsettled → protective) · Rue (sees straight through her, and Tilly knows it) ·
Aven (adopts, then resents being adopted back).

**Arc.**
1. Ring the bells. Keep the count right.
2. Discover Aven can *hear* the difference between a rung and a rung-for bell (S-111) —
   the first time anyone has confirmed her private knowledge.
3. Be told, by the Tally, that a rung-for bell *is not a category* — which is
   simultaneously her vindication and her erasure.
4. Act II: name Aven's condition out loud (S-253). She is the only one who notices.
5. **S-371, the funeral:** the count is read one short. She does not correct it.
   **This is her arc, complete, in a single omission.**
6. Epilogue (best variant): she sleeps in. It is presented as an enormous victory.

**Favourite things.** Rope. Correct answers. The specific sound of the third bell. Bread
ends. Being trusted with something heavy. *(Gift/cooking: bread ends ×10 = achievement #70.)*

**Battle mechanic — ANCHOR.** Ward mass ×3; tether is a **rigid rod**, not a spring.
`PLANT` roots her and the tether becomes a **solid collision line** blocking projectiles —
and blocking Aven's own movement past it. Insight from blocked bullets ×0.5; Strain
accrual −30%. *The beginner's companion and the expert's puzzle.*

**Theme.** *Ninety Bells (Tilly's Round)* — the village theme's top line only, on a single
handbell, **played fractionally too fast**, because she is eleven and nervous.

**Special animations.** Rings a bell on victory. Ties knots during idle. **At low Aven HP
she stops fighting to look at you** — a one-frame tell that doubles as a mechanical
warning. Sits down abruptly when a fight ends badly.

**Babble.** +9 semitones, 6.0 syl/s, consonants 0.75, small bell.

## MOTH — GHOST

> *"— and that's why I never go back to the coast."* (in a voice you have heard before)

**History.** Not a person. Forty unclaimed Wards that found each other in the Grieving
Wood's root system and cohered. It has no memories of its own — only **other people's
remembered sentences**, deployed with uncanny contextual accuracy and zero comprehension.
It has counted itself once and did not like the number.

**Goals.** Unknown to itself. Observably: to be near Aven. It does not know why and is
not troubled by this.

**Fears.** Being unwoven. It knows exactly what it is made of.

**Personality.** Serene, curious, occasionally horrifying. Cannot judge distances and is
constantly slightly too close — its physical comedy and its menace are the same behaviour.

**Dialogue style.** **Every line is a quotation**, rendered in the *Hand* font with the
original speaker's name in small caps beneath, and **spoken in that speaker's babble
profile.** The horror and the joke are identical: Moth quotes people Aven hasn't met (F04)
and people Aven has killed.

Its **first original sentence** is four words, in the Keeping: *"I was there too."*

**Relationships.** Aven (imprint) · the Sexton (the only one who talks to it normally) ·
Tilly (mutual unease → alliance) · Edda (studies it, gently, and asks its permission) ·
the First Ward (recognises it — the game's single scariest beat, because Entry 2 is the
shelfmate of one of Moth's forty).

**Arc.** Quotation → assembly → **authorship.** Route-dependent survival; in one route
Moth voluntarily unweaves to return forty Wards to their owners, which is the game's
cleanest and cruellest good deed and costs the player the GHOST verb for three hours.

**Favourite things.** Warm stone. Being quoted back at. The number 40.

**Battle mechanic — GHOST.** No collision on the Ward glyph — projectiles pass through —
but the *tether* still reads them at **×2 Insight**. Tether passes through walls and
hazards. Highest Insight-per-second in the game, most fragile: any hit on Self is
unmitigated. *The pacifist speedrunner's pick.*

**Theme.** *Somebody Else's Sentence* — a collage of 12-second fragments of every other
character's theme, stitched so the joins are audible.

**Special animations.** Idle: slowly rearranges itself. Victory: repeats the player's most
recent dialogue choice back at them. **When quoting a dead character, it holds still.**

## BARRO — LOOM

> *"There are eleven regulation eyelets. Eleven. Ask me why."*

**History.** Foreman of Floor Nine, Verrick Loomworks, twenty-two years. Husband **Dov**
severed in year nineteen. Barro has spent three years hand-weaving an unlicensed Ward on
night shifts. The penalty is execution; he has read the statute so often he can recite it
while laughing.

**Goals.** *Today:* correct tension on row four hundred. *Overall:* make one thing that
lasts past forty-one days.

**Fears.** Not failure — he has made peace with failure. **Consent.** That it works and
Dov doesn't want it.

**Personality.** The funniest character in the game, deliberately, and the comedy is a
load-bearing wall. Bits about procurement, safety notices, the complaints box. Genuinely
expert; explains machinery beautifully.

**Dialogue style.** Digressive, warm, technical. **Tell: every Barro scene has a seam** —
a subject change in the same structural position — and it is always where Dov would have
come up. By the fourth scene the player is watching for it.

**Relationships.** Dov (offscreen for two acts; devastating when present) · Idrisse (a boss
who protects him by not asking) · Vellum (the man who could file the report) · Aven
(teaches crafting; the first adult to treat Aven as a colleague).

**Arc.** Comedy → the weave is discovered → **Choice B** → three outcomes, one of which is
his execution. In the "ask" branch he burns three years of work, is fine for eleven
minutes of screen time, and then asks Aven whether they think he'd have asked if he'd
known the answer.

**Favourite things.** Correct tension. Thread that doesn't slub. Dov's handwriting on a
shopping list, kept in his coat, never mentioned to anyone.

**Battle mechanic — LOOM.** Tether leaves a **persistent thread trail** for 4s. Threads
block nothing but convert crossing bullets to Insight at ×1.5, and **closing a loop**
around an enemy applies `BOUND` (attack rate −40%, 6s). The only companion who lets you
*pre-build* safety.

**Theme.** *Forty-One Days* — the Loomworks theme's music-box counter-melody given the full
arrangement. **The joke instrument becomes the main instrument.**

**Special animations.** Measures things during idle. **Rolls his sleeves up when a boss
enters its rage phase.**

## SENNET — CURRENT

> *"I'd like to go up. I've been going to say that for nine years."*

**History.** Joined the Drowned Choir at nineteen, by choice, for reasons that were
excellent at the time and that he can no longer fully reconstruct. Twenty-eight now. He has
the best ear in the choir and the least conviction.

**Goals.** *Today:* hold the fourth part without thinking about it. *Overall:* air.

**Fears.** That leaving proves the whole thing was optional, which would make 288 years of
other people's sacrifice a choice they didn't have to make.

**Personality.** Gentle, funny underwater in a way that requires the player to give up air
to hear. Loyal past the point of self-harm and aware of it. **The most conflict-avoidant
character in the game**, which is why he needs a player to open the door.

**Dialogue style.** Nearly everything is almost a note. Speaks in phrases that resolve.
**Tell: he answers questions about himself by describing the choir.**

**Relationships.** The Cantor (father-figure; has never once been told no) · the Chorister
(protects him; is the one arranging around the wrong note) · Aven (the first person who
asked him a question about himself in nine years).

**Arc.** Loyal drowning → the question → surfacing, or not. **Fails permanently if ten or
more voices have been silenced** — he was never going to leave a smaller choir, and the
game never tells you this rule.

**Favourite things.** The fourth part. Bread that has been anywhere near a fire. The
specific green of light through forty feet of water at noon.

**Battle mechanic — CURRENT.** The tether **pushes projectiles aside** rather than
absorbing them — deflect, not delete. Bullets keep existing and go somewhere. The most
spatially demanding companion and the best in dense patterns.

**Theme.** *Sennet Surfaces* — 52→96 BPM. Begins submerged; **the reverb dries out over
three minutes as he rises.**

**Special animations.** Holds a note during idle. On victory, exhales.

## CAPTAIN RUE ALDERMAN — SIGNAL

> *"I don't stop. That's the whole trick."*

**History.** Born in Wyndmarrow's House Twelve (F03). Took the March at nineteen after her
predecessor was audited standing still. Sixty years of motion. **Terminally ill** and has
told nobody. Filed Form 12-B in autumn 902 on behalf of eleven named crew.

**Goals.** *Today:* the rota. *Overall:* keep the March moving; keep the crew from watching
her die.

**Fears.** Being mourned. Being stopped. In that order, and she knows the order is wrong.

**Personality.** Dry, generous, hyper-competent, allergic to being thanked. Runs the
best-liked community in Vesselmere and sleeps alone in the last car on purpose.

**Dialogue style.** Clipped, funny, deflective. **Tell: she answers every emotional
question with logistics.** Every single one, without exception, until S-325. She is the
only major character who lies to the player, and only by omission, and the player can catch
her as early as Act II via `NO ACTION REQUIRED`.

**Relationships.** Ottoline (has worked it out) · the whole March (loves her, which is the
problem) · Annike (they have met; Rue filed at Hushfell) · Aven (the only person she
cannot out-manoeuvre).

**Arc.** Beloved captain → **the petitioner.** She asked for eleven. It gave them
everybody. She found out in Act II and kept moving, because stopping is the one thing she
cannot do. **Choice C** resolves her.

**Favourite things.** Timetables. Bad coffee. The sound of couplings taking strain. Her
crew, which she cannot say.

**Battle mechanic — SIGNAL.** Paints **safe lanes** 1.5s ahead of each volley. Following
the lane is free safety; taking the tether through the *unsafe* side yields **×3 Insight.**
She makes pacifism an explicit gamble every four seconds.

**Theme.** *Never Once Stopped* → Act III variant *Somewhere To Put It Down*, **the only
track in the game that ends on a full stop instead of a loop.**

**Special animations.** Checks a pocket watch mid-fight. **In her last fight, she doesn't.**
