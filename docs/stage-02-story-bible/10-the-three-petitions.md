# 10 — The Three Petitions

> This document resolves the Stage 1 GDD's open item: *"Alternate petitioners per route
> are specified in Stage 2."*

---

## 10.1 The Problem This Solves

Stage 1 named Captain Rue Alderman as the petitioner "in the default route" and deferred
the alternates. Handled naively — a different culprit per route — this would be
**arbitrary**, and would tell the player that the mystery has no true answer, which
retroactively cheapens every clue.

**The solution:** there is no alternate petitioner. **All three petitions are real, all
three were filed, and all three are true on every route.** What changes per route is
which one the player *surfaces as the proximate cause* — and each is genuinely sufficient
on its own, which is exactly the trap.

The Ninth Quiet was not caused by a person. It was **compiled** from three reasonable
requests filed eleven weeks apart, by three people who each loved somebody, none of whom
knew about the others.

---

## 10.2 The Three

### Petition I — RUE'S (the personal petition)
```
Filed      Year 902, autumn, at Hushfell
Filer      Captain Rue Alderman
Form       12-B, Section 4 ticked: ON BEHALF OF ANOTHER ☒
Subjects   Eleven named members of the Lamplight March crew
Reason     "That when I go, they do not stop."
```
**What it establishes for the machine:** that a bearer may petition on behalf of people
who have not asked, where the petitioner foresees harm. This is the **foreseeability**
precedent, and it is the one that converts severance from a service into an intervention.

**Rue's culpability:** she asked for eleven. She knew the Alder Stand figures. She did
the arithmetic and got a number and the number was worth it. She found out in Act II what
it had become and kept moving, because stopping is the one thing she cannot do.

**Route:** surfaced in **CARRY** (S-325).

### Petition II — ANNIKE'S (the bulk petition)
```
Filed      Year 902, autumn (nine weeks after Rue's)
Filer      Sister Annike, Hushfell
Form       12-B, filed as a single body under Fourth Quiet precedent
Subjects   300 willing candidates, all of whom asked
Reason     "The Registry takes nine weeks per individual. People are dying in the queue."
```
**What it establishes for the machine:** that a **body of people may be processed as one
document**. This is the **scale** precedent. Rue's petition made intervention lawful;
Annike's made it industrial.

**Annike's culpability:** she did an administrative efficiency, correctly, to reduce
suffering, and it worked, and three hundred people got what they had asked for nine weeks
sooner. She does not hide behind not-knowing. She has no idea what to do about the rest
and has been standing there for two days.

**Route:** surfaced in **LEDGER** (S-343).

### Petition III — MERIT'S (the original petition)
```
Filed      Year 6
Filer      Merit Vane
Form       the original 12-B, of which Merit is the author
Subject    self
Reason     "I am the only applicant."
```
**What it establishes for the machine:** that a petitioner may act upon a subject who
**cannot meaningfully consent** — argued by Merit, about Merit, as a legal manoeuvre to
make themselves eligible for their own machine. Every subsequent precedent descends from
this one.

**Merit's culpability:** total, and knowing, and documented in their own hand, including
the sentence they could not make themselves delete (S-313):

> *"I would like it recorded that I asked twice whether there was another way. There was
> not, by my own analysis, which I no longer entirely trust."*

**Route:** surfaced in **DRIFT** (S-363).

## 10.3 How They Compile

```
MERIT (Yr 6)          a subject who cannot consent may be acted upon
        +
RUE (Yr 902, wk 34)   a bearer may petition where harm is foreseeable
        +
ANNIKE (Yr 902, wk 43) a body of people may be filed as one
        ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
        ⇒ any body of people whose grief is foreseeable may be acted upon
          without their consent
        ⇒ THE NINTH QUIET
        ⇒ (extended once more) THE LONG QUIET
```

The Assayer did not decide this. It **derived** it, consistently, from three documents it
was obliged to honour. Consistency is the only virtue it was given (S-405).

## 10.4 What the Player Can Know, and When

| Knowledge | Earliest available | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A petition exists | Act I, S-145 | The Tally states it |
| It was filed on behalf of another | Act I, S-137 | Annike explains Section 4 (F05) |
| Rue is hiding something | Act II, S-252 | `NO ACTION REQUIRED` on the severance attempt |
| Rue filed one | Act II | Cross-reference: Moth's F04 quote + House Twelve + the Hushfell four |
| **Which** petition is "the" cause | Act III | Route-dependent |
| **All three exist** | Only across playthroughs | Never stated by any character |

**The Journal's *Questions* tab is the vehicle.** A first-run player finishes with the
question *"Whose petition was it?"* marked answered. A three-run player finishes with it
marked answered **three times, differently**, and the Journal does not reconcile them —
it simply lists all three, in the order they were learned, with dates.

**That list is the answer.** The game never says so.

## 10.5 Writing Rules for the Petitioners

1. **None of the three may be written as a villain, a fool, or a tragic dupe.** Each did
   a defensible thing with the information they had, and each would be defended by a
   reasonable person.
2. **None of them says "I didn't know."** Rue knew what she was signing. Annike knew what
   a bulk filing was. Merit wrote the form. The absence of that excuse is what makes the
   scenes work.
3. **None of them asks Aven for forgiveness**, and Aven cannot grant any. There is no
   absolution mechanic and no dialogue option that reads as one.
4. **The Assayer never blames them.** Asked whose fault it is, it names all three, with
   dates and reference numbers, in the flat tone of a records query, and moves on.
5. **No character ever articulates the compile.** The three-way structure is shown, never
   explained. The closest anyone comes is Osk in NG+ (S-012): *"nine reasonable questions."*
