# 06 — Act II: The Ledger

**Runtime:** ~110 minutes · **Scenes:** S-201 → S-268 · **Regions:** Lamplight March (hub),
The Salt Ledger, Verrick Loomworks, The Drowned Choir
**Question the act answers:** *Where does it all go?*
**Answer it delivers:** *Nowhere. It's all still here.*

---

## 6.1 Structure — the Open Middle

Act II is **non-linear by design**. The three field regions can be tackled in any order.
The game tracks the order and uses it:

| Position | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| **First region visited** | Gets Vellum's *fresh* audit — competent, formal, funny (S-2xx-A) |
| **Second** | Vellum rattled; the apology (F09) |
| **Third (last)** | **The Slack Tether fires here, whichever region it is.** The companion lost is therefore route-dependent. |

The March is the hub between all three. Rue is met before any field region.

---

## 6.2 Lamplight March — the Hub

### S-201 BOARDING [MANDATORY]
The March does not stop. Boarding is a mechanic: you run alongside and Tilly does the
rope work, and she is *magnificent* at it, and it is the first time in the game she is
unambiguously good at something in front of adults.

### S-202 CAPTAIN RUE ALDERMAN [MANDATORY]
She is in the fifth car doing timetable arithmetic and does not look up for eleven
seconds.

> **RUE.** "You're the paperwork." *(still not looking up)* "Pell signalled ahead. She
> says you've got a reserved number from the founding, which is either the most
> interesting thing this year or a clerical error, and I've been doing this long enough
> to hope it's the error."
> **AVEN** [WRY] "Everyone keeps saying that."
> **RUE.** "Everyone's right. Sit down, you're blocking the aisle, and there's no aisle."

**Rue's writing rules:**
1. She answers emotional questions with logistics. Every time. It is a *tell*, not a
   quirk, and by Act III the player should have clocked it.
2. She is the funniest adult in the game after Barro and she uses it identically.
3. **She is the only major character who lies to the player**, and only by omission.
4. She is never cruel and never self-pitying, which is what makes Act III land.

### S-203 THE TWELFTH CAR [MANDATORY — plant, F10/★]
Aven walks the train. Twelve cars. The twelfth is sealed. Rue has the key on a chain and
does not mention it, and if asked:

> **RUE.** "Storage."
> **AVEN** [BLUNT] "It's a whole car."
> **RUE.** "It's a whole car of storage." *(and she moves on, pleasantly, and the
> conversation is over and nobody was rude)*

She also sleeps in the last car, alone. Ottoline mentions this as a fond eccentricity
in S-206. It is not one (F10).

### S-204 THE CREW [MANDATORY + OPTIONAL]
Twelve named crew. Cook Marrow-Pot (Quest 29 — the name gag, running all game). Deckhand
Ottoline (Quest 26). The Signalwoman (Quest 30 — route control). Coupling Duty (Quest 31 —
twenty minutes of unglamorous competence that makes the March a home; achievement #69).

**The March is the game's warmest place and it exists so that Act III has something to
break.**

### S-205 WHY WE DON'T STOP [OPTIONAL — Quest 27]
Alder Stand. A settlement that stopped for a season, in Year 744, and was audited to
nothing in nine days. Rue tells it flatly, as operational history.

> **RUE.** "Nine days. Four hundred people, all of them fine at the end of it, all of
> them very pleasant, none of them able to tell you why they'd built the place."
> **RUE.** "So we don't stop. That's the whole trick. There isn't a second part."

### S-206 OTTOLINE'S LETTER [OPTIONAL — Quest 26, FAILABLE]
Ottoline has written to Rue and can't deliver it. Aven can read it, deliver it, or leave
it. It says, in about forty words, that Ottoline has worked out Rue is dying and would
like permission to know.

**Delivering it** gets Ottoline thanked, and reassigned to the front car. Kindness as
exile. Rue does it without a flicker and it is one of the cruellest things she does.

### S-207 COOKING [OPTIONAL — 30 authored scenes]
The companion-conversation vehicle (GDD §07.6). Each companion × each act × ingredients
from regions visited. **This is where 11,000 words of the game's best character writing
lives.** Sample beats:
- Tilly, Act II, salt: she has never eaten anything from outside Wyndmarrow and is
  *deeply suspicious* and eats it all.
- Moth, any: does not eat. Sits. Quotes people at the table back at themselves, one
  beat late, which is either the funniest or worst thing depending on who's there.
- Barro, late Act II: explains the correct way to do a thing nobody asked about for four
  minutes and then stops mid-sentence and says *"He'd have liked this."*

## 6.3 The Salt Ledger

### S-211 SALT SIGHT [MANDATORY]
Saltwright Coom teaches strata reading. He is going blind from it and is completely
matter-of-fact.

> **COOM.** "Every Registry document from the founding to the drying. Pulped, settled,
> pressed. You're standing on the *paperwork*." *(taps his boot)* "Year four hundred,
> about. Bad year. Reads bitter."

### S-212 STRATA [MANDATORY + OPTIONAL — 18 collectibles]
The nine-century history in the wrong order. **This is the game's primary lore delivery
and it is entirely optional to complete.** Depth = date. Eight of the eighteen are the
precedents in §02.2.

Sample:
> **DEPTH 4 · YEAR 31.** *A household petition, granted. Six signatures, one subject.
> Marginal note in an unknown hand: "the subject was asked and said she'd think about it.
> Filed anyway. Query raised. Query withdrawn."*

### S-213 THE CITY UNDER THE CITY [OPTIONAL — Quest 15]
The Dune Registrar has been alone for sixty years, still stamping, in a buried archive.
He is content. He is not mad. He has a system.

> **REGISTRAR.** "Somebody has to be here when they come back for it." *(beat)* "They
> won't. That's not the point. The point is somebody's here."

**He is a deliberate mirror of the First Ward**, planted six hours early, and players who
make the connection on a replay report it as the moment the game got very large.

### S-214 FILED UNDER NOTHING [MANDATORY — Quest 16, ★]
Requisition 12-B/1441 has no category. Following it here yields a **second** requisition
number and the first hard evidence of the Keeping: a manifest line reading
`TRANSFERRED — HOLDING` rather than `DISPOSED`.

**Aven's realisation is a single line and the game does not underline it:**
> **AVEN** [any tone] "...It doesn't say destroyed."

### S-215 THE TENTH STRATUM [MANDATORY — BOSS B4]
A creature of sediment from a year that hasn't happened. Its existence proves the Long
Quiet is **scheduled**, not hypothetical (F06).

**UNKNOT:** *"It is holding a date."* The filing stamp is six months out. It comes apart
with something like relief.

### S-216 COOM'S LAST READING [OPTIONAL — Quest 14, FAILABLE]
Take him on the dig and it finishes his eyes. He asks. He is glad. It is Aven's fault
and it is what he wanted, and the game holds both.

## 6.4 Verrick Loomworks

### S-221 FLOOR NINE [MANDATORY]
Vertical factory. The first artificial light in the game. Barro is mid-monologue when
Aven arrives and does not stop for four minutes.

> **BARRO.** "Eleven regulation eyelets. *Eleven.* Ask me why. No — don't, because the
> answer's a committee, and the committee's dead, and the eyelets remain."

**Barro's writing rule:** the comedy is real comedy and it is also a wall. Every Barro
scene has a seam in it where he changes subject, and the seam is always in the same
place, and by the fourth scene the player is watching for it.

### S-222 FORTY-ONE DAYS [MANDATORY — Quest 18] ★ **F07 PAYOFF**
Why synthetic Wards fail on day 41. Investigating the failure yields the answer nobody
in the building has: **they are not synthetic.** Verrick is processing filed Wards out of
the Keeping through a back channel, at half-life, which is why they degrade.

Three people know: Idrisse, a dead procurement clerk, and now Aven.

**This is the Act II midpoint reveal and it lands as an industrial-safety finding, not a
revelation.** No music sting.

### S-223 WHAT IDRISSE DOESN'T SAY [OPTIONAL — Quest 20]
Barro's boss knows about the illegal weave and reports nothing. Complicity as an act of
love. She never says so. Asked directly:

> **IDRISSE.** "Floor Nine hits quota."
> **AVEN** [BLUNT] "That's not what I asked."
> **IDRISSE.** "Floor Nine hits quota." *(and that is the entire answer, and it is
> generous, and it is the most she can afford)*

### S-224 THE COMPLAINTS BOX [OPTIONAL — Quest 21]
Bolted shut. Full. Two hundred complaints, none about work.

> *"The night shift is fine. I would like to know who to speak to about my brother."*
> *"Not a complaint. Nowhere else to put it."*
> *"I have written this eleven times and I keep taking it back out."*

### S-225 DOV [MANDATORY — Quest 19 setup]
Barro's husband. Severed year nineteen of twenty-two. He is at home. He is kind. He asks
after Barro's back, because Barro's back has been bad for a decade and Dov has retained
the fact and lost the reason he cared.

**Barro is not in this scene.** He has never once brought anyone home. The player meets
Dov alone and the entire scene is Dov being pleasant while Aven works out why Barro's
seam is where it is.

### S-226 THE WEAVE [MANDATORY — Choice B]
Three years of night shifts, finished. Eleven regulation eyelets, hand-set.
**Does he ask Dov if he wants it?** (GDD §06.5.B — all three outcomes.)

**Writing note on the "Ask" branch — the game's most-cited scene.** Dov says no. He is
kind about it. He is *right*, and the game gives him the better argument:

> **DOV.** "You've made something beautiful and I can see that it's beautiful. I can't
> want it." *(beat)* "That's not me being stubborn. I've tried to want it, since you
> asked. There's nowhere in me for it to go."
> **BARRO.** "It'd give you the — "
> **DOV.** "It'd give *you* the — " *(and he stops, because he's not cruel, and the
> stopping is worse than finishing)*

Barro burns it. He is fine. **He is fine for eleven minutes of screen time**, and then he
asks Aven, in a completely level voice, whether they think he'd have asked if he'd known
the answer, and the player gets four tones and none of them help.

**Barro seats. Third tether: LOOM.**

### S-227 QUILL'S PROMOTION [OPTIONAL — Quest 22, FAILABLE, ⇄]
The auditor's apprentice. Encourage him into the job or out of it. **The pipeline that
makes Vellums**, and if the player pushes him in, he appears in Act III with a lanyard and
Vellum's exact cadence, and it is horrible, and it is the player's doing.

## 6.5 The Drowned Choir

### S-231 FORTY VOICES [MANDATORY]
The region's ambient music is 41 NPCs. Held Breath is a traversal resource *and* a
speech cost — you can only talk underwater by giving up air.

### S-232 THE CANTOR [MANDATORY]
Believes the harmony is the last uncut thing in Vesselmere. **Is correct.**

> **THE CANTOR.** "Two hundred and eighty-eight years. Not one of us severed. Do you
> know why?" *(beat)* "Because a thing that is being *used* cannot be filed. We are in
> use. Continuously. That is the entire theology."

### S-233 SENNET [MANDATORY — Quest 23, FAILABLE, ⇄]
Wants to leave and cannot say so in front of the choir. Loyalty as a cage.

If ten or more voices have been silenced by the time the player addresses this, **he
stays** — he was never going to leave a smaller choir. The game does not tell you this
rule. It is discovered by comparison.

**Sennet seats (if he surfaces). Fourth tether: CURRENT.**

### S-234 TEACHING THE CHORISTER [OPTIONAL — Quest 24]
A severed singer sings tunelessly. Nobody has told him. Do you?

**Both answers are defensible and the game refuses to prefer one.** Telling him: he
thanks you, adjusts, and sings correctly and joylessly forever. Not telling him: forty
people continue to arrange themselves around one man's wrong note out of love, every day,
for the rest of the game, and you can hear them doing it.

### S-235 THE PART THAT ISN'T SUNG [OPTIONAL — Quest 25, ★]
Forty-one seats. Forty singers. The 41st part is scored, notated, and rests (F08).

> **THE CANTOR.** "It was left for whoever was coming."
> **AVEN** [CAREFUL] "Who was coming?"
> **THE CANTOR.** "Nobody came." *(beat)* "We sing the rest anyway. In tempo. So the
> place is kept."

### S-236 THE CANTOR [MANDATORY — BOSS B6]
Damage is measured in silenced voices, permanently, on the save file (GDD §08.2).
**Nothing warns the player.** Players notice the thinner ambient mix roughly twenty
minutes later, in a different room, and the realisation is the point.

## 6.6 The Act II Twist & Set Piece

### S-251 THE MANIFEST [MANDATORY] ★ **THE ACT II TWIST**
Wherever the third region ends, the requisition trail resolves. `TRANSFERRED — HOLDING`,
sixty-one thousand four hundred times.

**Severed Wards are not destroyed. There is a vault. It can, in principle, be given back.**

Aven's line is short and the scene ends on it:
> **AVEN** [any] "It's all still here."

### S-252 THE SLACK TETHER [MANDATORY] ★ **THE SET PIECE**
```
Fires   During the final region's boss, whichever it is
Cast    Aven, current companion, Vellum
```
Mid-fight. No cutscene. No pause.

The Assayer severs Aven's seated companion — reaching *into* Aven to do it. The tether
whitens and drops. The HUD ability icon greys out and reads `— NOBODY —`.

**The fight continues.** Most players die here. That is intended and it is survivable.

**Vellum stops.** He looks at what he has just been party to, and his next attack has
visible hesitation frames, and he says the thing that begins his arc (F09):

> **VELLUM.** "That wasn't — " *(and he checks his own paperwork, and it was)* "That was
> within scope."

**Companion-specific outcomes:**

| Seated | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| **Tilly** | Severed. She is *fine*. She keeps helping. She is competent and pleasant and she stops correcting people, and the absence of the corrections is how you know. |
| **Barro** | Severed. He keeps making jokes. The jokes stop having a seam in them. |
| **Sennet** | Severed. He surfaces. It doesn't matter to him now, and he does it anyway, because he'd said he would. |
| **Rue** | **Cannot be severed here** — she has already been processed (§10). The attempt returns `NO ACTION REQUIRED` and Aven sees the words. *This is how a sharp player catches her in Act II.* |
| **Moth** | **The severance fails.** Moth is made of unclaimed Wards and has nothing to cut. Vellum has no procedure. The fight ends in an argument with a form and it is, briefly, hilarious. |

### S-253 AFTER [MANDATORY]
The March, that night. Nobody says the right thing. Rue's scene here is her best in the
act and it is entirely logistics:

> **RUE.** "Right. So we re-berth them in the fourth car, and I'll want the rota redone,
> and — " *(she stops. She actually stops.)* " — and I'm sorry. That's not a rota problem.
> I know it isn't."

**Aven's absence of an absence.** Aven tries twice to describe what losing a seated
companion felt like, given that Aven has never had a Bearing to lose. Both attempts fail.
Tilly — or whoever remains — names Aven's condition out loud for the first time.

### S-254 THE NINE DOORS [OPTIONAL — Quest 35, ★]
By now the player has passed at least six keyholeless doors (F14). The Journal's
*Questions* tab has been quietly accumulating them since Wyndmarrow.

### S-255 EXIT TO ACT III [MANDATORY]
The Signalwoman plots the March's course (Quest 30). **The Eight are checkpointed here
and the route resolves** (GDD §06.7) — CARRY, LEDGER, or DRIFT.

The route is never named on screen. The player finds out because Act III is somewhere
else.

**Act II exit state:** route · companion roster (permanently reduced) · voices silenced ·
`flag:keeping_known` · `flag:rue_no_action` (if seen) · music stems permanently muted
