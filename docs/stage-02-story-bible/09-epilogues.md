# 09 — Epilogues: All Five Endings

Every ending gets a **walkable** epilogue, not a slideshow. The player moves through
Vesselmere and sees what they made. Assembled from 14 hand-written variant modules
(GDD §10.3).

**Universal rules:**
- No narrator. No text crawl. No "and so, Vesselmere…"
- The player controls Aven throughout, at walking pace, with no combat and no fail state.
- Every epilogue can be ended early by the player walking to a specific place. Nobody is
  held hostage.
- **No ending is scored, ranked, or labelled.** The word "true" appears nowhere in-game.

---

## 9.1 RETURN — the True Ending

**Mechanism.** Aven gives Merit back their heart. Merit, having a reason to care what
happens to anyone, files one last 12-B **on behalf of everyone, requesting continued
regard** — asking for grief to be given back. No precedent exists. The Assayer makes one.

### The immediate scene (S-409, ~5 min)
Merit remembers Ilsabet and Corrin. Nine hundred years arrives at once in a body that has
been a filing system since before anyone alive was born.

**It is not a triumph.** Merit screams for a long time. The hall stops sorting. The
falling forms land and nothing catches them.

Aven stays. The player cannot leave, and the only input available is *stay*, and this is
the game rhyming with HUSH deliberately — the player who has already found HUSH will
recognise the shape of it and the player who hasn't will find it in the archive later.

### The epilogue walk (~14 min)
Region by region, as the returns land. **It is not clean.** The Keeping's contents are
nine centuries past half-life (§1.4) and most returns are partial.

| Beat | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Wyndmarrow** | Halden gets Odile back and does not recognise the man he became without her. He goes looking for the eleven objects Aven sorted in S-104. **The game names the ones you discarded.** |
| **Tilly & Odile** | Odile looks at Tilly and *knows* her. Tilly runs. It takes three days. The reunion is not shown — the player sees the third day, from a distance, from the bell tower. |
| **Dov** | Refuses his. He has had four years of being a person who doesn't have it and he has built a life in there. Barro says *"right"* and means it. They stay married. |
| **Ossa** | Gets back a son's death she chose to be rid of. Thanks nobody. Is not grateful. Is alive. |
| **Sennet** | Surfaces. |
| **The Drowned Choir** | Whatever voices remain sing. The 41st part is still silent. |
| **Rue** | Mourned by her crew *while alive*, badly and loudly and for weeks. The March stops for the first time in sixty years, for one afternoon, for her. Then it goes again. |
| **Vellum** | Files the return paperwork. Sixty-one thousand four hundred entries. It takes him the rest of his life and he is happy. |
| **Merit** | Dies, ordinarily, in a chair, having been a person for **eleven days**. Aven is there. It is undramatic. |

### Final shot
Wyndmarrow at dawn. The Bell Count is read aloud — **whatever number the save file
earned**, spoken by Tilly if she lives, by the boy with the ladder if she doesn't.

The game does not say whether the number is good.

## 9.2 THE LONG QUIET — the Bad Ending

**Mechanism.** Aven severs the Assayer. The machine stops.

### The ninety seconds where it's a win
The Ninth Quiet ends. Nobody is ever severed again. The bells ring the next morning and
the count holds. The game lets this be a victory, with warm music, for **ninety seconds**.

### The cost
The Keeping was *inside* the machine. Sixty-one thousand four hundred stored Wards go out
like a light in the same instant. Every severance in history becomes permanent.

### The epilogue walk (~11 min)
Vesselmere functioning perfectly. The bells ring. The March runs on time. Everyone is
polite. *Ninety Bells* plays with however many instruments the save file left it.

**Every NPC is kind to Aven and none of them ask where they've been.** The player walks
the whole world and cannot get a single person to be anything other than pleasant.

**Last line**, from Tilly, warmly, in the tower:
> **TILLY.** "Sorry — have we met? You've got a face like someone."

**Writing rule:** no scolding, no "you monster," no red filter, no funeral. The player
did the thing the game called `SEVER` for six hours. The game shows the result and shuts
up.

## 9.3 THE LEDGER — the Neutral Ending

**Mechanism.** Aven takes Merit's place. Someone has to keep it, and cruelty and chaos
are both worse.

### The immediate scene
Aven sits in the chair. It is the right size.

Aven runs the Assay **better**: severance by petition only, three-day cooling-off, full
disclosure, no generalisation from precedent. It is Annike's rite scaled to a
civilisation, and it is genuinely good policy, and the game presents it as such.

### The epilogue (~9 min) — told entirely in forms
The player reads paperwork. That is the whole epilogue and it is the point.

| Form | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Petition 1** | Tilly Brack, annual, requesting a visit. **Denied** on procedural grounds, by a system Aven designed and cannot exempt anyone from. Filed again the next year. And the next. Eleven of them, and the handwriting gets older. |
| **Petition 2** | Barro, licence application for hand-weaving. **Approved.** He is the first licensed knotter in nine hundred years. |
| **Petition 3** | Moth, requesting to be unwoven. **Held pending clarification of whether Moth is a person.** Still pending. |
| **Petition 4** | Ottoline, requesting reassignment back to the fifth car. Approved, posthumously, by four days. |
| **Petition 5** | Vellum, requesting to be assigned as Aven's clerk. Approved. He brings tea. He is not allowed to stay. |
| **The last form** | Filed by Aven, to Aven, requesting **one hour off.** Stamp reads `PENDING`. It has read PENDING for some years. |

If Osk was saved on NG+ (S-011), there is a twelfth form: Osk's, which is a **query**,
correctly filed, that suspends Aven's own directive pending clarification — and the last
image is the machine stopping, mid-sort, because one old filing clerk knew exactly which
box to tick.

## 9.4 THE KEEPING — the Secret Ending

**Mechanism.** Aven opens the vault without dealing with the Assayer at all. Nine hundred
years of stored love comes out at once, into a world with no plan for it, and **bonds go
to the nearest available chest.**

### The Flood (~14 min, the longest epilogue)
Vesselmere spends a year in a condition nobody names. People carry strangers' griefs.
They weep in the street for the dead of people they never met. Society fails —
practically, logistically, visibly. The March **stops**, and nobody audits it, because the
Assayer is standing in a field receiving nine hundred years of returned grief through a
system built to file it, and **it cannot file this.**

### The turn
Slowly, over the epilogue's second half: it holds.

People learn to carry what isn't theirs. A woman in Wyndmarrow grieves a man from
Verrick she never met and goes to Verrick to find out who he was. Half the country does
something like this. The roads fill up.

**This is the only ending where Vesselmere becomes something new instead of something
restored.** The score (*The Flood*) begins as chaos and resolves into a single communal
melody that nobody is leading.

### Final shot
The Assayer, in a field, still standing, still receiving. Vellum beside it with a
clipboard and no idea what to write. Neither of them leaves.

## 9.5 HUSH — the Hidden Ending

**Gate.** The First Ward resolved (either way) **and** the final menu refused three times.

Detailed in §08.2 S-418. The epilogue is ten minutes of two people sitting on a floor.

**Nothing is fixed.** The Ninth Quiet continues. The world outside is not shown, not
once, and the player never learns what happened to it.

The credits roll over the two of them, still sitting. *A Tuesday in the Year Eleven* —
solo felted piano, one take, audible pedal, audible breath, **one wrong note left in.**

**Why this ending exists:** Pillar III. Grief is not a villain, and not every problem is
a boss. Someone stayed. That is the whole content of the ending and it is enough.
