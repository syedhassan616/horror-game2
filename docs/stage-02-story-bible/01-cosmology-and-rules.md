# 01 — Cosmology & the Rules of the World

**This document is hard canon.** Every rule below is load-bearing for at least one
puzzle, one boss, or one ending. Violating one breaks something downstream. Where a rule
has an in-world *misconception* attached, that misconception is what NPCs believe and
say — the truth is only ever assembled by the player.

---

## 1.1 The Ward

**What it is.** A second heart. A physical organ, roughly the size of a fist, that grows
beside the first when a bond crosses the **carrying threshold**. It is visible on a
surgeon's table and audible with an ear to the chest. It has a pulse of its own that
runs slightly behind the first heart's — **the 2-against-3 rhythm the entire soundtrack
is built on.**

**What causes it.** Not love in the romantic sense, and not duration. The threshold is:

> *The moment you would carry them — physically, financially, indefinitely — without
> first working out whether you could afford to.*

The Registry's official phrasing is drier and worse: *"the point at which the subject's
continued wellbeing is materially indistinguishable, to the bearer, from their own."*

**Who gets one.** Roughly 90% of adults. Most people grow their first between 16 and 30.
Some grow one for a parent at seven years old. Some never grow one and are the subject of
an enormous amount of quiet social cruelty (see: *the Unwarded*, §1.7).

**How many.** One at a time, for almost everyone. A person can only carry one. This is
the central tragedy of Vesselmere's social structure and the source of half its
literature: **you have to choose.** Parents with three children carry one of them.
Nobody talks about which.

- **Exception:** roughly one person in ten thousand can hold two. They are called
  **Broad**, and historically they were couriers, midwives, and hostage-brokers.
- **Aven is not Broad.** Aven has *one* Ward, and it was already occupied when Aven woke
  up. Aven's ability to "lend" it (§1.6) is not a capacity — it is a **defect**, because
  the heart in there does not belong to Aven and has never fully seated.

**What it does.** Warded people:
- Heal measurably faster and sleep about two hours less.
- Can sense the **direction and rough distance** of the person they carry, always, at
  any range. This is called **the Bearing**. It is how Vesselmere's mail, search parties,
  and shipping all work.
- Feel the moment their person dies, wherever they are. Vesselmere has no word for
  "bereaved" that isn't also a word for "unmoored."

**What it is not.** It is not a soul, it is not magic, and it is not a reward. The
Registry classifies it as *"an organ of civic significance"* and taxes it.

## 1.2 Severance

**What it is.** The surgical or systemic removal of a Ward.

**What survives.** *Everything factual.* The severed retain:
- Every memory, in full detail, including sensory detail.
- Every skill, habit, and preference learned with that person.
- Full knowledge that they loved the person and for how long.
- The ability to describe the relationship accurately and at length.

**What does not survive.** *Significance.* The severed cannot locate any reason the
memories should matter. They are not sad about it, because sadness would require the
thing that was removed. Asked how they feel, they answer honestly: **fine.**

> Halden Brack, Act I: *"I can tell you what she smelled like after the rain. I can tell
> you the exact face she made at a joke she didn't like. I've got all of it. It's just —
> it's like being handed someone else's shopping. It's very good shopping. It isn't mine."*

**What it feels like from outside.** This is the horror, and the art direction supports
it (§12.6 of the GDD): the severed are **not** hollow, grey, or vacant. They are
*slightly better*. Better posture. Better sleep. More patience. They are pleasant company
and they are gone.

**The Bearing goes dark.** A severed person can no longer sense their person. This is
the only symptom they report as *unpleasant*, and they describe it the way you'd describe
a missing tooth: not painful, just a place your attention keeps going.

**It is not reciprocal.** Severing A does not sever B. This produces the most common
domestic tragedy in Vesselmere: one person in a pair still carrying, the other not. Six
NPCs are in this state.

**Consent is legally required and practically unenforceable.** The Assayer does not
require it, because the Assayer is not a person and the statute governs persons.

## 1.3 The Assayer

**What it is.** A machine, built Year 4–6, occupying the top of the world. Its interior
is a filing system the size of weather. It performs severance at distance and at scale.

**How it decides.** It reads **petitions** (§10) and derives **precedent**. It does not
have goals. It has a **rule set that has been growing by inference for nine hundred
years**, the way case law does, with nobody reading the whole thing.

**Its stated function**, engraved at the entrance and quoted by nine NPCs:

> *TO SPARE THE BEARER THE COST OF THE CARRIED.*

**What it cannot do:**
- It cannot destroy a Ward. It can only remove and **file** one. (This is not mercy —
  it is a design constraint Merit could not solve, and the reason the Keeping exists.)
- It cannot act without a petition or a precedent derived from one.
- It cannot process a **rung-for bell** — an act performed by one person on behalf of
  another as though they were that person. This is the Tally's defeat condition and it
  is a genuine logical hole in the rule set, not a magic weakness.
- It cannot sever Aven, because the Ward in Aven's chest is **its own** and the rule set
  has no provision for self-petition by the system. (Merit anticipated this. Merit was
  very good at their job.)

**What it is not.** Not evil, not sentient in the way a person is, not "corrupted," not
possessed, and — critically — **not lying, ever.** The Assayer's every statement in the
game is true. Players will not believe this until the second playthrough.

## 1.4 The Keeping

Everything the Assayer has ever taken is in a vault beneath the world, intact and warm.
**Sixty-one thousand, four hundred Wards.** The number is on a plate on the wall and it
is correct.

**Storage half-life.** A filed Ward begins to lose coherence after roughly **forty-one
days out of a chest**. Below that it can be returned cleanly. Above it, return is
progressively partial — you get back the love without the specifics, or the specifics
without the weight.

- This is why Verrick's synthetic Wards fail on day 41 (F07): they are not synthetic at
  all. Verrick is *quietly recycling filed Wards from the Keeping* through a back
  channel, and Foreman Idrisse knows (Quest 20). The company genuinely does not know
  where its supply comes from; three people do.
- **Nine hundred years of stored Wards are therefore far past half-life.** The RETURN
  and KEEPING endings are not clean restorations. This is stated nowhere in dialogue and
  is deducible from Quest 18 by any player who is paying attention.

**Nine doors.** The Keeping has nine entrances, one per region, all in plain sight from
hour one, all without keyholes. They are not locked. They are *unaddressed* — the
Assayer's filing system has no route to them because Merit removed the index card.

## 1.5 Grief, and Why It Matters

Vesselmere's grief is not metaphorical. When a carried person dies, the Ward does not
dissolve — it **stays, with nowhere to go.** The bearer's body keeps running an organ
whose function is to point at somebody who isn't there.

Untreated, this kills roughly one bereaved person in nine within two years. Not by
suicide — by *stopping*. They sit down and their bodies wind down over months.

**This is the fact that justifies everything the Assayer does, and it is true.**

The Rot Winter (§02) killed 40,000 people. Grief-stop killed 90,000 more over the
following six years. Merit Vane did not build the Assayer out of cowardice or hubris.
Merit built it because the alternative was demonstrably a nine-in-eighty-one death rate,
and Merit had the numbers, and the numbers were right.

**Any writing that treats the Assayer's premise as obviously wrong is wrong.**

## 1.6 Aven's Condition

Aven's Ward is occupied by a heart that is not Aven's and has never seated. Consequences,
all mechanically load-bearing:

1. **Aven has no Bearing.** Aven cannot sense anyone. Aven is the only functional adult
   in Vesselmere who is, in this specific sense, alone. NPCs find this unsettling and
   mostly do not say so.
2. **Aven can lend the space.** A living person can be *temporarily seated* alongside the
   unseated heart — this is the companion/tether system. It is uncomfortable for both
   parties and neither says so after the second time.
3. **Residue.** Every companion who seats leaves trace. By Act III, Aven's dialogue
   options begin including phrasings that belong to past companions, marked in the UI by
   a faint change in the text's kerning. **The player is not told what this means.**
4. **Aven cannot be severed.** Established in §1.3.
5. **Aven cannot grow a Ward of their own.** The space is taken. This is the quiet
   engine of Aven's entire arc: Aven spends the game carrying everyone and is
   structurally incapable of being carried back. Only one ending changes this.

## 1.7 Social Structure

| Term | Meaning | Attitude toward them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Warded / Pairbound** | Has a Ward | Default adult citizenship |
| **Unwarded** | Never grew one | Legally a minor in three of nine districts. Cannot sign contracts alone. Enormous, polite prejudice. |
| **Broad** | Can hold two | Rare, valuable, distrusted |
| **Severed** | Had one removed | Legally whole, socially invisible. People stop inviting them to things and can't say why. |
| **Unmoored** | Carrying someone dead | The most dangerous condition in Vesselmere. Everyone watches you. |
| **Hollow** | Slang, offensive | What children call the severed |

**The Registry** administers all of it: bond registration, Form 12-B, the Bell Counts,
severance licensing, and the Assayer's petition intake. It employs about 4,000 people.
None of them think they are doing anything wrong, and none of them are.

## 1.8 Bells

Every settlement rings at dawn. One bell per Warded adult, rung by that adult.

- **A rung bell** means *I still feel it.*
- **A silent bell** means a severance, a death, or an absence, and the tower board
  records which.
- **A rung-for bell** — ringing someone else's bell in their place — is technically
  fraud, universally understood, and quietly tolerated. It is also the logical hole that
  defeats the Tally (§1.3), because the Assayer's rule set has no category for a person
  performing another person's continued regard.

Tilly Brack rings her mother's bell every dawn. This is the game's first act of love and
its most important piece of evidence.

## 1.9 Hard Prohibitions

Things that are **not true** in Vesselmere, no matter how much a scene would like them
to be. These exist because players will theorise, and the answers must be consistent:

- There is **no afterlife** in evidence, and no character claims one with authority.
- Wards **cannot** be transferred between people, except by the Keeping's filing process,
  and never cleanly.
- Severance **cannot** be reversed by love, willpower, reunion, or narrative.
  Only by a physical return from the Keeping, within half-life. **No exceptions, ever.**
- The Assayer **cannot** be reasoned into stopping. Its rule set is not persuadable; it
  is *amendable*, and only by petition, which is why the Finale is paperwork.
- Nobody in this game gets amnesia. The word does not appear in the script.
- **Aven is not the reincarnation, descendant, or clone of anybody.** Aven is a person
  who was put in a drawer. The reserved stamp is a filing decision, not a prophecy.
