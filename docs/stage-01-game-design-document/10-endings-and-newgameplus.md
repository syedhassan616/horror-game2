# 10 — Endings, Epilogues & New Game+

---

## 10.1 The Final Choice

The Assayer's shell is open. Merit Vane is inside — small, very old, and holding
nothing. Aven has Merit's heart. The game presents **four verbs**, plus a fifth that is
not presented.

No confirmation prompt. No music sting. No camera push. The four words sit on a black
field and the game waits as long as you need.

```
        RETURN            SEVER            ASSUME            OPEN
```

The fifth is **doing nothing, three times.** The game will re-present the menu twice
after a long silence. On the third, it stops asking.

---

## 10.2 The Five Endings

| Ending | Verb | Gate | Tone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **RETURN** — *True* | RETURN | Mercy ≥ 60, Hope ≥ 55, Annike alive, ≥2 companions present | Devastating, and then hopeful |
| **THE LONG QUIET** — *Bad* | SEVER | Wrath ≥ 50 or Assayer killed | Quiet. Not punishing. Worse than punishing. |
| **THE LEDGER** — *Neutral* | ASSUME | Default; reachable from any state | Ambiguous, dignified, lonely |
| **THE KEEPING** — *Secret* | OPEN | All nine doors found, First Ward encountered | Overwhelming. The loudest ending. |
| **HUSH** — *Hidden* | *(refuse three times)* | First Ward **resolved** + refuse ×3 | Silent. Ten minutes with almost no text. |

---

### 10.2.1 RETURN — the True Ending

Aven gives Merit Vane back their heart.

Merit remembers their child. Nine hundred years of it, arriving at once, in a body that
has been a filing system since before anyone alive was born. **It is not a triumph.**
Merit screams for a long time. The Assayer's hall stops sorting, and the falling forms
land, and nothing catches them.

And then Merit — who now has, again, a reason to care what happens to anyone — **files
one last form.** Form 12-B, on behalf of another, requesting continued regard. It is the
only petition the machine has ever received that asks for grief to be *given back*.

The Keeping opens on its own. Nine hundred years of severed bonds go home.

**The epilogue** walks Vesselmere region by region as the returns land, and it is not
clean:
- Halden Brack gets his wife back and does not recognise the man he became without her.
- Odile Brack looks at Tilly and knows her, and Tilly runs, and it takes three days.
- Some returns are refused. Dov's is one of them, and the game respects it.
- Ossa gets back a son's death she chose to be rid of, and thanks nobody.
- Sennet surfaces.
- Rue is mourned by her crew *while she is alive*, badly and loudly and for weeks.
- Merit Vane dies, ordinarily, in a chair, having been a person for eleven days.

Final shot: Wyndmarrow at dawn. The Bell Count is read aloud. Whatever number your save
file earned is the number you hear. **The game says it in a child's voice and does not
tell you whether it's good.**

### 10.2.2 THE LONG QUIET — the Bad Ending

Aven severs the Assayer. The machine stops.

**The Ninth Quiet ends.** Nobody else is severed, ever again. This is, on its face, a
win — and the game lets it be one for about ninety seconds.

Then: the Keeping was inside the machine. Nine hundred years of stored bonds go out like
a light. Every severance in history becomes permanent and irrecoverable in the same
instant. Sixty thousand people who could have been given back, aren't.

The epilogue is Vesselmere functioning perfectly. The bells ring. The March runs on
time. Everyone is polite. Wyndmarrow's theme plays with one instrument. Aven walks
through the whole world and every single NPC is kind to them and none of them ask where
they've been.

Last line, from Tilly, warmly: *"Sorry — have we met? You've got a face like someone."*

**Design note:** this ending contains no punishment, no scolding, and no "you monster"
text. The player did the thing the game called `SEVER` for six hours. The game simply
shows the result and lets it be quiet.

### 10.2.3 THE LEDGER — the Neutral Ending

Aven takes Merit's place. Someone has to keep the ledger; the alternative is either
cruelty or chaos, and Aven has spent the whole game learning that both are worse.

Aven sits in the chair. The chair is the right size. It was always going to be.

Aven runs the Assay *better*: severance by petition only, three-day cooling-off, full
disclosure, no generalisation from precedent. Annike's rite, scaled to a civilisation.
It is genuinely, defensibly good policy.

**The cost:** Aven stops being reachable. The epilogue is a series of forms — Tilly's
annual petition to visit, denied on procedural grounds by a system Aven designed and
cannot exempt anyone from; Barro's licence application, approved; Moth's request to be
unwoven, held pending clarification of whether Moth is a person.

The final form is filed by Aven, to Aven, requesting one hour off. The stamp reads
**PENDING**. It has read PENDING for some years.

### 10.2.4 THE KEEPING — the Secret Ending

Aven opens the vault without dealing with the Assayer at all.

Nine hundred years of stored love comes out at once, into a world with no plan for it.
It is not orderly. It is not targeted. **Bonds go to the nearest available chest.**

Vesselmere spends a year in a condition the game calls the Flood: people carrying
strangers' griefs, weeping in the street for the dead of people they never met, holding
each other because they cannot do anything else. Society fails. The March stops for the
first time in sixty years and nobody audits it, because the Assayer is standing in a
field, receiving nine hundred years of returned grief through a system built to file it,
and it cannot file this.

And then — slowly, over an epilogue that takes fourteen minutes — it holds.

People learn to carry what isn't theirs. It is the loudest, messiest, most
uncomfortable ending, and it is the only one where **Vesselmere becomes something new
instead of something restored.** Playtests split hard on whether it's the best ending.
That split is the design goal.

### 10.2.5 HUSH — the Hidden Ending

Requires: the First Ward resolved, and refusing the final menu three times.

The fourth time, the menu doesn't appear. Aven sits down on the floor of the Assay
opposite Merit Vane, and the game hands the player nothing to do.

**Ten minutes. Almost no text.** You can move. You can walk out. Most players don't.

Merit talks — not about grief, not about the machine. About a Tuesday in the year 11
when their kid wouldn't eat a specific vegetable. Eleven minutes of a very old person
remembering one ordinary afternoon they have not been able to *feel* for nine centuries
and can now only *describe*.

And Aven — who has been carrying Merit's heart the entire game — **doesn't give it back
and doesn't keep it.** Aven just stays.

The credits roll over the two of them, still sitting. Nothing is fixed. The Ninth Quiet
continues. And at the very end, one line, unvoiced, in Aven's own text box — **the only
line in the game with no tone tag**:

> *"I don't know either. I'll wait with you."*

This ending changes nothing about the world. It is entirely about the fact that someone
stayed. It exists because Pillar III is real: **grief is not a villain, and not every
problem is a boss.**

## 10.3 Epilogue Variants

Each ending's epilogue is **walkable**, not a slideshow, and is assembled from **14
variant modules** driven by save state:

| Module | Variants | Driver |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wyndmarrow | 4 | Bell Count bands |
| Tilly | 5 | Survival, Odile's state, ending, Resolve |
| Barro & Dov | 4 | Choice B outcome |
| Annike & Hushfell | 3 | Choice A + whether she lives |
| Rue & the March | 5 | Choice C + petition disposition |
| Moth | 4 | Woven / unwoven / authored / silent |
| Vellum | 4 | Defected / audited / promoted / Quill replaced him |
| Sennet & the Choir | 4 | Voices remaining (0 / 1–10 / 11–30 / all 41) |
| Edda & the Commonplace | 3 | The page |
| Ash Garden | 3 | Both Armies outcome |
| The Keeping | 3 | Opened / sealed / destroyed |
| The First Ward | 3 | Severed / waited-with / never met |
| Aven's Voice | 5 | Dominant tone |
| Named dead | dynamic | Each of the 11 possible NPC deaths gets an authored line |

**Combinatorics:** ~2.1 million distinct epilogue readings; **every module is
hand-written**, none procedural. A player will not see the same epilogue twice across
three playthroughs, and every sentence in it was written by a person.

## 10.4 Ending Discovery & Respect

- The game **never** shows an "endings collected 2/5" screen during play. That checklist
  turns endings into laundry.
- After your first completion, the title screen changes subtly and permanently — the
  bell in the logo is silent, or rung, depending on how you finished.
- The Journal's *Questions* section carries over into NG+ **unanswered**, so a returning
  player is chasing their own curiosity, not a list.
- Achievement names for endings are non-descriptive (§16) so the achievement list is not
  a spoiler map.

## 10.5 New Game+

NG+ is not a difficulty mode with better gear. It is **a different reading of the same
text.**

### What carries over
- The skill tree (respec free), all Keepsakes, the Journal, the *Questions*, hidden
  nodes including **Still Waiting**.
- **Not** equipment, Marks, or capacity — Act I must still be survived at 20 HP.

### What changes

| System | NG+ change |
| --- | --- |
| **Enemies** | 18 types are replaced by their **Late variants** — same family, patterns built from the *back half* of the pattern pool. The Clerical family now files *you* by name. |
| **Boss dialogue** | Every boss has a full NG+ dialogue set. The Tally opens with your final Bell Count from last run. The Assayer opens with: *"You've read the file."* |
| **Vellum** | Remembers. All four fights change. If he defected last run, he is *waiting for you* in Act I, out of uniform, and is a companion for two regions. |
| **Moth** | Quotes **your previous playthrough's dialogue choices**, attributed to `AVEN`. This is the single most-discussed feature in playtest. |
| **The First Ward** | Available from NG+ start (still requires the chain). Gains **6 additional patterns**, family *"Nine Hundred and One."* |
| **The Undersleep** | Opens without the twelfth-car quest. The Version Of You mirrors your **previous run's** build, not this one's. |
| **New content** | 4 NG+-exclusive quests, 1 NG+-exclusive region wing (the Assay's *Archive of Refusals* — every petition the Assayer ever declined), 9 new Keepsakes. |
| **Chapter select** | Unlocked after any completion. Jump to any act with a curated save state, for ending-hunting without a full replay. |
| **NG+ difficulty** | **The Long Quiet** difficulty unlocks (§05.10). Bosses gain a 6th phase. |

### The NG+ thesis

The Assayer's line in NG+ — *"You've read the file"* — is the whole design. On a second
run the player knows what everyone is holding, which means **UNKNOT becomes trivial and
the game knows it.** So NG+ shifts the difficulty from *diagnosis* to *execution*: you
know the answers; now survive long enough to say them.

And it adds one thing a first run cannot have: on NG+, when Osk is severed in the
Prologue, **you can stop it.** It takes a specific, difficult, entirely unmarked action
in the first ninety seconds of the game. Most players will fail it the first time they
try. He is *right there*, and you know what's coming, and the game has spent an entire
playthrough teaching you that you can't fix everything.

You can fix this one.
