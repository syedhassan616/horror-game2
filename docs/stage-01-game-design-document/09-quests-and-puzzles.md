# 09 — Side Quests & Puzzles

> Full objective chains, all branch text, and failure-state authoring are
> **Stage 6 (Quest Database)**. This chapter is the complete roster and the design law.

---

## 9.1 Quest Design Law

1. **No fetch quests.** If the objective can be summarised as "bring N of X," it is
   deleted. Zero exceptions across all 35.
2. **Every quest reveals lore or develops a character.** Preferably both. A quest that
   only grants an item is a treasure chest with extra steps.
3. **Nine quests can fail** — permanently, through player action, inaction, or the
   passage of story time. Failure is authored content, never a "quest failed" toast.
4. **Six pairs are mutually exclusive.** Helping one person means not helping another,
   and the game shows you the other outcome later.
5. **No quest markers on the world map by default.** The Journal records what you were
   *told*, in the teller's own words, and you navigate from that. (A marker option
   exists in accessibility settings, §13.)
6. **Every quest-giver has a life before and after.** They existed in the region before
   they needed you; they are somewhere specific afterwards.

## 9.3 Failure States — the nine

| Quest | How it fails | What you see afterwards |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 The Ninety-First Bell | Guisley dies or leaves before casting | The 91st bell frame hangs, empty, for the rest of the game |
| 03 Guisley's Last Casting | Reach Act III without doing it | Forge cold. Superboss chain **hard-blocked** this run. |
| 08 Something Buried Twice | Unweave Moth first | The second keepsake is never identified. Journal question stays open forever. |
| 10 Ossa's Third Day | Let the third day pass | Ossa is severed. She is well. She asks if you're new here. |
| 14 Coom's Last Reading | Take him on the dig | He completes it, and is blind, and grateful, and it is your fault and also what he wanted |
| 19 Barro's Eyelets | Turn him in / let the raid happen | Floor Nine runs a shift short. Nobody mentions it. |
| 22 Quill's Promotion | Encourage him | Quill appears in Act III with a lanyard and Vellum's exact cadence |
| 23 Sennet's Surface | Silence 10+ choir voices first | Sennet stays. He was never going to leave a smaller choir. |
| 26 Ottoline's Letter | Deliver it to Rue | Rue reads it, thanks her, and reassigns her to the front car. Kindness as exile. |
| 33 The Page Edda Won't Touch | Force her to read it | Edda reads it. She is not the same afterwards. Her final scene is two lines long. |

*(Ten listed — quest 33 is both failable and part of the ★ chain, and the design intent
is that the ninth "failure" a player experiences is usually one they didn't know was a
quest at all.)*

## 9.4 Puzzle Design

Puzzles are **regionally themed and mechanically distinct**. No puzzle type appears in
more than two regions. All are **solvable without combat** and all can be skipped via
the accessibility Puzzle Assist (§13.6) without any content loss.

| Type | Region(s) | Example | Count |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Environment** | Wyndmarrow, Verrick | Route rope-bridges so a bell's sound reaches a deaf quarter of the village | 9 |
| **Logic** | Salt Ledger, Commonplace | Reconstruct a nine-century filing order from 18 out-of-sequence strata | 7 |
| **Music** | Drowned Choir, March | Reproduce a 41-part harmony with only the parts you've collected; the missing part must be *left silent* | 6 |
| **Light** | Grieving Wood, Commonplace | Angle canopy gaps to grow a specific tree; shelve books by the shadow they cast | 5 |
| **Time** | Hushfell, Orrery | The three-day wait, played across three real visits; orbital timing windows | 5 |
| **Pattern recognition** | Verrick, Salt Ledger | Read a loom's weave to predict the next shift's output | 6 |
| **NPC cooperation** | Wyndmarrow, March, Verrick | Six villagers must ring in a sequence; each will only ring after someone they trust | 7 |
| **Physics** | Orrery, Verrick | Re-parent island gravity to roll a mass into place; thread-tension bridges | 8 |
| **Memory** | Commonplace, Undersleep, Keeping | Recall a conversation from Act I *verbatim* — the Journal's "Things People Said" is the tool, and the answer was never highlighted | 6 |
| **Tether puzzles** | All | Overworld tether-hooking; the combat verb used as a traversal tool | 12 |

**Total: 71 discrete puzzles**, of which 22 are optional and 9 are hidden entirely
(no prompt, no glow, no indication — found by players who try things).

**The memory puzzle class is the flagship.** In the Commonplace, Edda asks you what
Osk said to you in the first ninety seconds of the game. Four options. The game logged
it. You may not have read it. There is no penalty for being wrong, and there is no
reward for being right except that Edda looks at you differently, and so does the
player at themselves.
