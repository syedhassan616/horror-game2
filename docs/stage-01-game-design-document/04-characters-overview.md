## 4.2 Major Character Dossiers

Each dossier follows the required schema: **history · goals · fears · personality ·
dialogue style · relationships · arc · favourite things · battle mechanic · theme ·
special animations.**

### 02 · TILLY BRACK — *"The count has to be right."*

- **History.** Eleven. Wyndmarrow-born. Her mother Odile was severed in the first week
  of the Ninth Quiet. Odile still lives in the house, still cooks, still calls Tilly
  "sweetheart" — with the exact warmth of a woman greeting a neighbour's child. Tilly
  rings her mother's bell every dawn so the tower board doesn't record a silence.
- **Goals.** Keep the count right. Be believed. Not be sent somewhere kinder.
- **Fears.** That the count was *always* wrong and nobody checked. Being carried
  instead of carrying.
- **Personality.** Relentlessly literal. Corrects adults. Enormous competence in a
  narrow band (bells, ropes, knots, weather) and none outside it. Not precocious — she
  is a child doing an adult's job badly and refusing help.
- **Dialogue style.** Short declaratives. Refuses metaphor: *"That's not what a heart
  is. A heart is a muscle. I've seen a sheep's."* Interrupts constantly (`[INTERRUPT]`
  heavy). Never says how she feels; says what needs doing instead.
- **Relationships.** Aven (adopts, then resents being adopted back) · Odile (unbearable)
  · Guisley the bell-founder (grudging apprenticeship) · Moth (deeply unsettled by it,
  then protective) · Rue (sees straight through her).
- **Arc.** Ring the bell → discover Aven can *hear* the difference between a rung bell
  and a rung-for bell → be told the count doesn't matter → the finale, where the count
  is the thing that matters. Her ending state is route-dependent and includes one where
  she stops ringing and *sleeps in*, which is presented as an enormous victory.
- **Favourite things.** Rope. Correct answers. The specific sound of the third bell.
  Bread ends. Being trusted with something heavy.
- **Battle mechanic — ANCHOR.** Ward mass ×3. Tether is a **rigid rod** rather than a
  spring. `PLANT` roots her; while planted, the tether becomes a **solid collision line**
  that physically blocks projectiles (and blocks *your* movement past it — the skill is
  in what you fence off). Insight gain from blocked bullets is halved; Strain accrues
  30% slower. **The beginner's companion and the expert's puzzle.**
- **Theme.** *Ninety Bells (Tilly's Round)* — the village theme, but only the top line,
  played on a single handbell, slightly rushed. She is playing it fractionally too fast
  because she is eleven and nervous.
- **Special animations.** Rings a bell during victory. Ties knots in idle. When
  Aven is at low HP she *stops fighting to look at you* — a one-frame tell that is also
  a mechanical warning.

### 03 · MOTH — *"—and that's why I never go back to the coast."* (in a voice you've heard before)

- **History.** Not a person. A creature woven from unclaimed Wards that found each other
  in the Grieving Wood. It has no memories of its own, only other people's remembered
  sentences, which it deploys with uncanny contextual accuracy and zero comprehension.
- **Goals.** Unknown to itself. Observably: to be near Aven. It does not know why and
  is not troubled by this.
- **Fears.** Being unwoven. It knows what it is made of and has counted itself.
- **Personality.** Serene, curious, occasionally horrifying. Physical comedy — it
  cannot judge distances and is constantly slightly too close.
- **Dialogue style.** **Every line is a quotation** — displayed in a distinct font, with
  the original speaker's name in small caps beneath. The horror and the joke are the
  same thing: Moth quotes people Aven hasn't met yet (F04), and people Aven has
  killed. Its **first original sentence** is the payoff of its arc and is four words
  long.
- **Relationships.** Aven (imprint) · the Sexton (the only one who talks to it normally)
  · Tilly (mutual unease → alliance) · Edda Grist (studies it, gently) · the First Ward
  (recognises it, which is the game's single scariest beat).
- **Arc.** Quotation → assembly → authorship. Whether Moth survives is route-dependent;
  in one route Moth *voluntarily unweaves* to return 40 Wards to their owners, which
  is the game's cleanest and cruellest good deed.
- **Favourite things.** Warm stone. Being quoted back at. The number 40.
- **Battle mechanic — GHOST.** Moth has **no collision** — projectiles pass through the
  Ward glyph — but the *tether* still reads them, at **×2 Insight**. The tether can be
  dragged through walls and hazards. Highest Insight-per-second in the game and by far
  the most fragile: any hit on Self is unmitigated. **The pacifist speedrunner's pick.**
- **Theme.** *Somebody Else's Sentence* — a collage track built from 12-second fragments
  of every other character's theme, stitched at the seams so the joins are audible.
- **Special animations.** Idle: slowly rearranges itself. On victory: repeats the
  player's most recent dialogue choice back at them.

### 04 · BARRO — *"There are eleven regulation eyelets. Eleven. Ask me why."*

- **History.** Foreman of Floor Nine, Verrick Loomworks, twenty-two years. His husband
  Dov was severed in year nineteen. Dov is still at home and still kind. Barro has been
  hand-weaving an unlicensed Ward on night shifts for three years. The penalty is
  execution and Barro has read the statute so many times he can recite it while laughing.
- **Goals.** Finish the weave. Get the eyelets right. Make one thing that lasts past 41 days.
- **Fears.** That it works and Dov doesn't want it. (Not that it fails. He's made peace
  with failure. He has not made peace with *consent*.)
- **Personality.** The funniest character in the game, deliberately. Bits about
  procurement, safety notices, the complaints box. All of it is a load-bearing wall.
- **Dialogue style.** Digressive, warm, expert. Explains machinery beautifully. Changes
  subject the instant anything approaches Dov, with a visible seam.
- **Relationships.** Dov (offscreen for two acts, devastating when onscreen) · Idrisse
  (a boss who protects him by not asking) · Vellum (the man who could file the report) ·
  Aven (mentor-ish; teaches crafting).
- **Arc.** Comedy → the weave is discovered → whether he is allowed to *ask Dov* is one
  of the game's three hardest player choices, and one of the nine failable quests.
- **Favourite things.** Correct tension. Thread that doesn't slub. Dov's handwriting on
  a shopping list, which he keeps in his coat and has never mentioned to anyone.
- **Battle mechanic — LOOM.** The tether leaves a **persistent thread trail** for 4
  seconds. Threads block nothing but **convert any bullet that crosses them to Insight
  at ×1.5**, and can be **woven into a closed shape** — closing a loop around an enemy
  applies `BOUND` (attack rate −40%) for 6s. High skill ceiling, spatial, and the only
  companion who lets you *pre-build* safety.
- **Theme.** *Forty-One Days* — the Loomworks theme's music-box counter-melody, given
  the full arrangement. The joke instrument becomes the main instrument.
- **Special animations.** Measures things during idle. On a boss's rage phase, he
  *rolls his sleeves up.*

### 06 · CAPTAIN RUE ALDERMAN — *"I don't stop. That's the whole trick."*

- **History.** Born in Wyndmarrow house 12, the one with no photographs (F03). Took the
  March at nineteen after her predecessor was audited standing still. Sixty years of
  motion. She is terminally ill and has told no one.
- **Goals.** Keep the March moving. Keep the crew from watching her die.
- **Fears.** Being mourned. Being *stopped*.
- **Personality.** Dry, generous, hyper-competent, allergic to being thanked. Runs the
  best-liked community in Vesselmere and sleeps in the last car, alone, on purpose (F10).
- **Dialogue style.** Clipped, funny, deflective. Answers emotional questions with
  logistics. **She is the only major character who lies to the player**, and the lie is
  a lie of omission the player can catch as early as Act II with the right Journal
  cross-reference.
- **Relationships.** Ottoline (a crew member who has figured it out) · the entire March
  (which loves her, which is the problem) · Annike (they have met; Rue filed the form
  at Hushfell) · Aven (the only person she can't out-manoeuvre).
- **Arc.** Beloved captain → the petitioner. **She filed Form 12-B on behalf of another**
  (F05) — asking that her crew be spared mourning her. The Assayer, a machine of
  precedent, generalised her one request into the Ninth Quiet. She did not know. She
  found out in Act II and kept moving.
- **Favourite things.** Timetables. Bad coffee. The sound of couplings taking strain.
  Her crew, which she cannot say.
- **Battle mechanic — SIGNAL.** Rue paints **safe lanes** — a 1.5s telegraph of where
  the next volley *won't* be. Following the lane is free safety; ignoring it and taking
  the tether through the *dangerous* side yields ×3 Insight. She is the companion who
  makes the pacifist route an explicit risk/reward gamble every four seconds.
- **Theme.** *Never Once Stopped* — and its Act III variant *Somewhere To Put It Down*,
  the only track in the game that ends on a full stop instead of a loop.
- **Special animations.** Checks a pocket watch mid-fight. In her last fight, she
  doesn't.

### 07 · VELLUM — *"I am going to need you to hold still, and also to stop being like that."*

- **History.** The Assayer's field clerk. Not a construct — a person, employed, with a
  lanyard. Fought **four times** across the game.
- **Arc across four fights.** (1) Flawless, funny, bureaucratically menacing. (2) Rattled;
  apologises to a creature mid-audit and corrects himself (F09). (3) Openly bad at his
  job; his attack patterns literally develop *hesitation frames*. (4) He has grown a
  Ward — for **you** — and the fourth fight is either his defection or his last audit,
  depending on how you have treated him. **Every mercy you have shown Vellum is
  recorded and read out in fight four, by him, as an itemised list.**
- **Battle mechanic.** Vellum is the **only enemy with a tether of his own.** His mirrors
  yours in real time, one companion-behaviour behind. Fighting him is fighting your own
  last hour of play.
- **Theme.** *A Clerk, Ascending* — same 8 bars each fight, transposed up a semitone
  each time, arrangement degrading as it rises.
- **The design intent.** Vellum is the character the community will make fan art of. He
  is funny for two hours before he is anything else.

### 08 · SISTER ANNIKE — *"Nobody here was taken. Everybody here asked."*

- **The design brief:** *write the antagonist the player cannot beat in an argument.*
- Runs the Hushfell rite: three-day cooling-off, full disclosure, tea, no fee, and she
  performs it herself so no one else has to carry it. She has severed 300 people. She
  keeps every register entry.
- **Fears.** That one of the 300 would have changed their mind on day four.
- **Her fight** is optional and **defensive only** — she never attacks. She parries,
  withdraws, and talks. Killing her is possible, trivially, and **permanently closes
  the True Ending.** The game does not warn you. The Register of Willing gains a
  three-hundred-and-first entry, in her hand, dated that day.

### 09 · EDDA GRIST — blind archivist of the Commonplace.
Grants Aven **READ** — the ability to sense emotional weight, which becomes the
diagnostic tool for UNKNOT (§05) and the key to nine puzzles. Reads by touch; there is
exactly one page she will not touch (F11). Dialogue style: precise, dry, unhurried,
occasionally devastating. *"You want me to tell you it means something. I catalogue.
I don't appraise."*

### 10 · THE CANTOR — leads the Drowned Choir.
Believes the harmony is the last uncut thing in Vesselmere and is correct. Damage in
this fight is dealt in **silenced voices**, and every voice you silence is gone from
the region's ambient mix for the rest of the save file. Winning by force leaves the
Drowned Choir permanently, audibly emptier for the remaining four hours of the game.

### 11 · MERIT VANE / THE ASSAYER — see §08.5 and §10.
The engineer who solved grief and became the solution. Severed themselves first and
hid their heart in the inventory. Speaks with enormous patience and no malice. Never
raises its voice, including during its rage phase — the rage phase is expressed as
*speed and efficiency*, not anger, which is worse.

### 12 · THE FIRST WARD — see §08.6.
The first second-heart that ever formed, still in the Keeping, still waiting for a
person who died nine hundred years ago. It does not attack out of malice. You are
between it and the door.

## 4.3 The NPC Framework — 46 Named NPCs

**Every NPC must pass the three-question test** or they are cut:
1. What do they want *today*?
2. What do they know that the player can't get elsewhere?
3. How do they change if the Bell Count drops / a companion dies / the player is `BLUNT`?

**Every named NPC has:**

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `memory_flags` | What they've seen you do; drives greeting selection |
| `conversation_stages` | ≥3 authored stages; they never repeat a barked line |
| `severance_state` | `whole` / `severed` / `synthetic` — changes portrait, VO-less speech rhythm, and idle |
| `reading_bias` | Which of the Eight they weight when reading you (§06) |
| `topic_memory` | Last topic discussed; enables "you asked me about that already" |
| `schedule` | Time-of-day position; NPCs are *somewhere*, not standing at a post |

**NPC distribution:** Wyndmarrow 9 · Grieving Wood 4 · Hushfell 6 · Salt Ledger 5 ·
Verrick 7 · Drowned Choir 5 · Lamplight March 12 · Orrery 4 · Commonplace 5 ·
Ash Garden 4 · (some appear in multiple regions; 46 unique).

**The rule against filler:** there are no "wandering villager" NPCs with a single
line. Ambient population is handled by *silhouettes and crowd sprites* that cannot be
talked to. If it has a talk prompt, it has a name, a want, and three stages. This is a
hard content gate, checked per region in QA.
