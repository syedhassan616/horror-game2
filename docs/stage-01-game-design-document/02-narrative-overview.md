# 02 — Narrative Overview

> Full scene-by-scene breakdown, all branches, and every line of dialogue live in
> **Stage 2 (Story Bible)** and **Stage 7 (Dialogue Database)**. This chapter is the
> spine: premise, structure, twists, themes, and the foreshadowing ledger.

---

## 2.1 The Premise

In **Vesselmere**, a bond that crosses the *carrying threshold* grows a physical organ:
a **Ward**, a second heart. It is not romance — it is the moment a person becomes
someone you would carry. Parents grow Wards for children. Soldiers for the person
beside them. A baker grew one for a dog and the Registry argued about it for a decade.

Wards are the foundation of the civilisation. Warded people are stronger, sleep less,
heal faster, and — critically — can *feel each other's location*. Vesselmere's roads,
its mail system, its search-and-rescue, its entire concept of adulthood, are built on
Wards.

**Nine hundred years ago**, during the Rot Winter, grief killed more people than the
plague did. Whole villages of the bereaved simply stopped. A civic engineer named
**Merit Vane** built a machine to sever Wards *before* the person died — to take the
love out preemptively, so the survivor would keep living. It worked. The Rot Winter
ended. Merit Vane was thanked, and then quietly reviled, and then forgotten.

The machine — **the Assayer** — was never switched off. It has been performing severances
at a low, invisible rate for nine centuries, always defensibly, always at the margins.

This winter, it started severing **thousands**. This is the **Ninth Quiet**.

**The severed do not forget.** That is the horror. Halden Brack can describe his wife's
laugh in ten sentences. He simply cannot locate any reason it should matter. He is
unfailingly polite about it.

## 2.2 The Protagonist

**AVEN** *(default name; fully player-renameable, 12 chars)*

Aven is **not silent.** Aven has a portrait with 14 expressions, speaks in every scene,
and their personality is *authored by the player* through tone choices rather than
selected from a list at character creation.

- **Origin:** Wakes in the Unclaimed Vault, catalogued as inventory. No memory of
  arriving. Two hearts beating.
- **The problem:** Aven's second heart is **occupied but unattributed**. There is
  someone in there. The Registry has no record of who.
- **The gift:** Because the second heart is unattributed, Aven can **temporarily lend
  it** — let a living person occupy it. This is why companions are mechanically your
  tether: they are *literally inside your chest* during a fight.
- **The cost:** Every companion who occupies Aven's Ward leaves residue. By Act III,
  Aven is carrying fragments of everyone they've travelled with, and the game starts
  quoting past companions in Aven's own dialogue options.

**The Voice System.** Dialogue choices are tagged by *tone*, not content:
`WARM · WRY · BLUNT · CAREFUL · QUIET`. Aven's portrait, idle animations, walk cycle,
and default responses drift toward the tone used most. By Act III, an Aven played
`BLUNT` stands differently, and NPCs greet them differently. This is cosmetic-plus:
it feeds **Trust** and **Compassion** (§06) but is never a stat check on its own.

**Aven's arc:** From *inventory* → *courier* → *carrier* → *the person who decides.*
The final ending choice is the first time Aven speaks without a tone tag.

## 2.3 Structure

| Act | Regions | Duration | Question the act answers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Prologue** | The Unclaimed | 20 min | *What is a Ward, and what does losing one look like?* |
| **Act I — The Ringing** | Wyndmarrow, The Grieving Wood, Hushfell | 75 min | *Who is doing this?* |
| **Act II — The Ledger** | The Salt Ledger, Verrick Loomworks, The Drowned Choir, Lamplight March | 110 min | *Where does it all go?* |
| **Act III — The Orrery** | The Orrery, The Commonplace, The Ash Garden *(route-variable)* | 90 min | *Who asked for this?* |
| **Finale — The Assay** | The Assay | 45 min | *What are you willing to hand back?* |
| **Epilogue** | Route-dependent | 10–25 min | *What did the world become?* |

### Prologue — The Unclaimed *(20 min)*

Aven wakes in a drawer. The **Unclaimed Vault** is a cold blue archive beneath the
world where Wards without owners are catalogued and shelved by a shuffling custodian
called **Ledgerman Osk** — patient, deaf in one ear, treats Aven as a filing error he
is *delighted* to have found, because it means paperwork he's never done before.

- **Teaches:** movement, interaction, the Journal, and the tether (Osk lends Aven his
  own long-dead Ward for the tutorial fight against a **Filing Error**, a creature made
  of misplaced index cards).
- **The gut-punch:** Osk is severed *during* the tutorial, mid-sentence. He finishes
  the sentence. He is still helpful. He does not understand why Aven is upset. The
  tether goes slack for the first time. **This is the game's thesis statement,
  delivered in 90 seconds.**
- **Exit:** Osk hands Aven a requisition slip and directions to Wyndmarrow, because
  "an unclaimed heart should be walked to the Registry, and I've got a great deal of
  re-shelving."

### Act I — The Ringing *(75 min)*

**Wyndmarrow** — a hill village of rope-bridges and bell-towers. Every morning
everyone rings their bell; a silent bell means a severance overnight. When Aven
arrives, eleven bells are silent. The village is pretending this is normal.

- Meets **Tilly Brack** (11), who rings her mother's bell *for* her so the count stays
  right. First companion. Tether: **ANCHOR**.
- **The Grieving Wood** — trees grow from buried keepsakes; each tree is a lost bond.
  Introduces environmental storytelling as a puzzle language, and **Moth**, a creature
  woven from unclaimed Wards who speaks only in other people's remembered sentences.
- **Hushfell** — a snowbound monastery where **Sister Annike** severs people *at their
  own request*, legally, kindly, with tea and paperwork and a three-day cooling-off
  period. The first major choice: Aven can shut the Hushfell rite down, endorse it, or
  walk away. All three permanently reshape the region.
- **Act I boss: THE TALLY** — the Assayer's counting-drone. Not evil. It is *auditing*.

**Act I twist:** The Assayer isn't malfunctioning. It is following an **updated
directive**. Somebody filed a request.

### Act II — The Ledger *(110 min)*

The search for the paperwork. Aven follows a requisition number across four regions.

- **The Salt Ledger** — a dead inland sea whose salt is powdered record-paper. Buried
  archive-cities. Reading the ground is literally reading history. Introduces the
  **Salt Reading** puzzle family.
- **Verrick Loomworks** — an industrial city that *manufactures* synthetic Wards for
  the severed. They don't work properly. **Barro** runs Floor Nine and is
  hand-weaving an illegal one for his severed husband.
- **The Drowned Choir** — a flooded amphitheatre. Forty singers chose to sink rather
  than be severed; their harmony still resonates under the water. **The Cantor**
  refuses to surface. This region is scored *diegetically* — the ambient music is the
  choir, and it changes based on how many of them you've convinced to stop singing.
- **Lamplight March** — a caravan-city that never stops moving, because stationary
  settlements are easier to audit. Hub for Act II. Party management, shops, the
  quest board, and **Captain Rue Alderman**.

**Act II twist (the big one):** Severed Wards are **not destroyed.** They are
*collected*. There is a vault — **the Keeping**. Everything taken can, in principle,
be given back.

**Act II's signature set piece — THE SLACK TETHER:** During the escape from Verrick,
the Assayer severs one of Aven's companions **mid-fight, without a cutscene.** The
tether whitens and drops. The player's dodge verb stops existing. The fight does not
pause. The HUD ability icon greys out with the label `— NOBODY —`. QA gate: this must
be survivable, and it must kill most players once.

### Act III — The Orrery *(90 min)*

Ascent to the Assayer's control layer.

- **The Orrery** — a shattered celestial machine of floating islands; the gravity of
  each island is set by what it is *holding onto*. Traversal puzzles built on
  attachment.
- **The Commonplace** — a library of every feeling anyone ever bothered to write down,
  tended by **Edda Grist**, blind, who reads by touching the emotional weight of a page.
  Optional-lore motherlode; the superboss chain runs through here.
- **The Ash Garden** *(route-variable)* — a battlefield where an entire army severed
  itself at once rather than watch each other die. Flowers grow that smell like
  people you have personally lost — the game names *your* dead, per save file.

**Act III twist:** The requisition that started the Ninth Quiet was filed by **a person
you have already met and probably like.** Their motive is love. They filed it to spare
one specific person, and the Assayer — being a machine of precedent — generalised.

> *Identity of the petitioner is route-dependent. In the default route it is
> **Captain Rue Alderman**, who filed to spare her own crew from mourning her
> terminal illness. Alternate petitioners per route are specified in Stage 2.*

### Finale — The Assay *(45 min)*

An infinite hall of ledgers. The Assayer is not a monster; it is a very tired clerk
the size of a cathedral. It is **polite**. It offers Aven tea, in the sense that a
machine can.

**The revelation:** Aven's unattributed second heart is **Merit Vane's**. Merit severed
themselves first — you cannot build the guillotine and keep your own neck — and hid
their capacity to love inside the system's own inventory, in case they ever wanted it
back. Aven has been carrying the villain's heart the entire game. That is why the
Assayer cannot destroy Aven, and why it has been *escorting* rather than hunting.

**The choice:** four verbs, presented without commentary, no confirm prompt:
`RETURN` · `SEVER` · `ASSUME` · `OPEN`

### Epilogue

Every ending gets a full walkable epilogue, not a slideshow. See §10.

## 2.4 The Themes

| Theme | Question | Where it's argued | Who argues the *other* side |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Grief as the price of love** | Is the price worth paying? | Hushfell, the Assay | Sister Annike — persuasively |
| **Consent** | Can you choose this for someone else? | Rue's petition, Barro's husband | Rue — desperately |
| **Memory vs. significance** | Is a fact you can't feel still a memory? | Osk, the Commonplace | Edda Grist — academically |
| **Mercy as violence** | When does kindness become an atrocity? | The whole game | The Assayer — calmly |
| **Custodianship** | Do we owe the dead our pain? | The First Ward, the Ash Garden | The First Ward — by waiting |
| **Bureaucracy as moral anaesthetic** | How do decent people run a machine like this? | Verrick, the Registry | Vellum — comically, then not |

**The thesis the game will not state out loud:** grief is not a bug in love; it is the
*shape love takes when it has nowhere to go*. The game shows this and never says it.

## 2.6 Callbacks & Memory

NPCs remember. Concretely, every named NPC carries a `memory_flags` set and a
`last_topic` reference, and conversation openers are selected against them.

- A shopkeeper you shortchanged in Act I greets you differently in Act III — and if
  you're playing `BLUNT`, they get there first.
- Tilly repeats Aven's own Act I phrasing back at Aven in Act III, having absorbed it.
  If Aven was played `WRY`, the line lands as a joke. If `QUIET`, it lands as an
  accusation. **Same line, different room temperature.**
- Any NPC severed on your watch greets you with perfect, warm politeness and zero
  recognition of shared history, forever. There is no cure at the individual level.
- The Journal auto-logs a **"Things People Said"** section — verbatim quotes, timestamped
  by region — which becomes a puzzle item in the Commonplace (§09).

## 2.7 Writing Standards

Enforced at review. A scene fails review if it breaks these.

1. **No exposition dumps.** Lore arrives as *someone's problem*. The nine-hundred-year
   backstory is never narrated; it is assembled from a salt stratum, a choir's missing
   part, a form, and one very old chair.
2. **Characters interrupt.** The dialogue system supports mid-line barge-in
   (`[INTERRUPT]` token) with visible truncation. Used for character, not for pace.
3. **Nobody speaks in themes.** If a character states the theme, the line is cut.
4. **Every NPC wants something in every conversation** — even if it's just to be left
   alone convincingly.
5. **Specificity over poetry.** "The gap in her teeth and the way she held a cup"
   beats "everything I loved about her."
6. **Comedy is structural.** Each region has a running gag that pays off *once*, hard.
   Wyndmarrow's is a bell-ringing competition. It pays off at a funeral.
7. **The player is never told how to feel.** No music sting on a moral choice. No
   reaction shot cut-in. The camera does not know it's a sad scene.
