HOLLOWLIGHT

A Descent in Six Lamps

In November 1911 the mining village of Grimhollow went dark in a single night. Nine hundred people. No bodies. The harbourmaster's final entry reads: “The lamps have gone out and we cannot find the man who lights them.”

You are Wren Halloway, hollow-mapper, sent to chart the mine before the cliff takes it. You carry your grandfather's lantern. Do not let it go out.

Contains darkness, sustained tension, sudden loud sounds and brief flashing light.
All of it can be reduced in Settings & Accessibility. Headphones recommended.

How to Play

Hollowlight has no combat. You survive by managing three things — light, sound and nerve — and by paying attention to what you read. Everything below is also in your Journal at any time (press Tab in game).

Controls

WASDMove. Arrow keys work too.
ShiftRun. Fast, and very loud.
CtrlCreep. Slow and near-silent.
QHold breath. Silent while still. Limited.
EInteract with whatever is prompted.
LLantern on / off. Burns oil while lit.
FFlare. Hold for a burst that drives them back. Costs oil fast.
RRecorder. Listen at a resonance point; reveals hidden chalk.
TabJournal — documents, characters, objectives, controls.
EscPause and settings.

The Three Meters

Oil

Your lantern is a clock.

Light drains oil. Flaring drains it fast. Refill at any street lamp — lamps are also where the game saves and where Dread bleeds away. Find oil cans in the dark places nobody wanted to go. At zero oil the lantern dies, and Dread climbs with nothing to hold it.
Dread

The dark gets inside.

Standing in darkness raises Dread. So does being seen, being hunted, and being right. High Dread warps what you see and hear — false doors, figures that aren't there, whispers using your name. Let it reach the top and Grimhollow adds you to its ledger.
Sound

The Wick Children are blind.

They hunt by ear. Running screams your position. Gravel and standing water betray you. Creeping is quiet; holding your breath while standing still makes you silent. A flare will drive one off — it will not kill it. Nothing here kills.
The Echo

Some rooms remember.

At a resonance point the recorder plays back what happened there. For a few seconds afterwards you will see the chalk marks the dead left behind — most puzzle answers are written on walls you cannot normally see.

Advice From Someone Who Went First

Read everything.

Every code in this game is written down somewhere. Nothing needs guessing. Documents are kept forever in your Journal.

Dark is a choice, not a mistake.

Lantern out means no oil burned and nothing can see your glow. It also means Dread. Trade deliberately.

Follow the small blue light.

If you are lost, she will show you. She has been trying to help for a hundred years.

Six sigils are hidden.

Chalk diamonds, one per chapter, always off the obvious path. All six unlock the third ending.

Chapter Select

Chapters unlock as you reach them. Jumping in grants the equipment and knowledge Wren would have by then.

Settings & Accessibility

Nothing here is a difficulty penalty. Play it the way it stays playable for you.

Presentation

Master volume
All sound is generated live in your browser — there are no audio files.
Screen brightness
Raise this if the dark rooms are genuinely unreadable on your display.
Text size
Scales all dialogue and interface text.

How fast dialogue types out. Far right prints instantly.

Comfort

Reduce flashing
Removes lightning strobes and the flare's white bloom.
Reduce camera shake
Stops the screen from breathing and lurching at high Dread.
Hallucinations
When off, high Dread will not spawn figures that are not there.
Audio subtitles
Prints a description of every important sound.

Difficulty

Mercy mode
Oil lasts twice as long, Dread climbs at half speed, and the Wick Children give you a second of grace before they take you. Story and puzzles are unchanged.
Puzzle hints
Adds a Hint button to every puzzle. It tells you the method, then the answer.

Story & Credits

Spoiler-free. The story below is the premise only — everything in the mine is yours to find.

The Premise

Grimhollow kept nine hundred souls and one lamplighter. On the night of the 3rd of November 1911 every lamp in the village went out at once, and by morning there was nobody left to complain about it. No struggle. No bodies. Nine hundred names in the parish ledger, every one of them crossed through in the same careful hand.

A hundred and thirteen years later the cliff is failing and the Ordnance Survey wants the mine charted before it drops into the sea. They sent you, because you map dead places for a living, and because you were the only applicant.

There is one more name at the bottom of that ledger. It is not crossed out yet. It is yours.

Built With

Light

Real-time shadow casting. Every light in the game builds a visibility polygon from the wall geometry each frame, so corners hide things and doorways throw shafts.

Sound

Every drone, heartbeat, footstep, whisper and note of the lullaby is synthesised live with the Web Audio API. No audio files ship with this game.

Everything else

One HTML page, one stylesheet, plain JavaScript. No engine, no build step, no dependencies. Open the file and it runs.

Inspirations, Gratefully

The oil that runs out and the sanity that doesn't come back — Amnesia: The Dark Descent. A town that is a confession — Silent Hill 2. Light as the only weapon — Alan Wake. Documents that are the real story — SOMA and Gone Home. The corridor that is never the same corridor twice — P.T. A blind thing that hears you breathe — A Quiet Place by way of Alien: Isolation. And a camera that sees the dead — Fatal Frame.
